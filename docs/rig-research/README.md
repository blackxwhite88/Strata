# Dual RTX 2080 Ti serving research

In November 2026 we measured Strata serving Qwen3.8-Flash-Next Heretic IQ4_XS on two RTX 2080 Ti cards. Native layer split, with stage trimming and piped MTP drafting (R2), became the preferred serving recipe. Tensor parallelism (TP) remained a research option. Larger conversation caches helped, but the number of parked entries mattered as much as the byte budget.

This is a record of one machine, including failed runs and corrected interpretations. The tables are historical measurements, not measurements made while preparing this bundle. No new engine behavior or executable default is introduced here. The final recipes were tested in short guarded windows; long unattended qualification remains open.

The rig had **modified 22 GB RTX 2080 Ti cards**, Turing SM75, an NVLink bridge, PCIe 3.0 x16 links, an Intel Xeon E5-2686 v4 and about 247 GB usable host RAM (8 × 32 GB registered DDR3, DDR3-1333). These are not stock 11 GB cards. Some early reports described 11 GB cards or 125 GiB RAM; those descriptions conflict with later live memory measurements. Their performance tables are retained as historical observations, with that correction. The recorded board was a HUANANZHI X99-T8. Window 2 recorded NVIDIA driver 595.99.02. TP builds used CUDA 12.4 (nvcc 12.4.131); the native recipe needed CUDA 13.3 runtime libraries. Those toolchains are separate.

## What happened

1. The [overnight plan](reports/rig-overnight-plan.md) became a [nine-arm battery](reports/rig-night-run.md): code, text, long-prefix reuse, 128k markers, images and concurrent requests. MTP, DFlash2, SM75 kernels, attention switches, context and cache sizes were compared. Later review found that its “aggregate decode” included prompt-reading time and that identical settings ran in different host states. The early weighted ranking is not a reliable causal ranking.
2. A [decode-only matrix](reports/rig-validate-run.md) separated prompt time from decoding. Stage trimming and piped MTP were useful native candidates. It initially attributed the unexplained slowdown to drafting and measurement effects. The [performance review](reports/strata-perf-review.md), [matrix review](reports/strata-test-review.md) and [scout](reports/strata-perf-scout.md) exposed the remaining host-state confounding. Short-prefix concurrent-prefill success did not predict later 128k correctness.
3. The [SM75 TP port](reports/strata-tp-build.md) built the native split engine and added CUDA peer transport. [Transport microbenchmarks](reports/strata-nvlink-tp-proof.md) showed that NVLink reductions were much faster than host exchange. The 16-column prompt recurrence fitted SM75's 64 KiB shared-memory limit. Those were component successes, not a complete serving qualification.
4. The [state investigation](reports/strata-tp-truth.md) reproduced FAST → SLOW → recovered inside one loaded process. A CPU-only copy probe slowed too; a register/L1 CPU loop did not. Ten minutes idle restored both memory bandwidth and decode. The [state review](reports/strata-fastslow-review.md) helped distinguish shared host-memory limits from per-card limits. The exact DIMM/controller physical trigger remains unknown.
5. Long concurrent TP prompts produced non-finite/range errors and, in one variant, Xid31 on both cards. [Fault analysis](reports/strata-tp-xid31-review.md), [plan review](reports/strata-tp-plan-review.md) and [fix review](reports/strata-tp-fix-review.md) separated hypotheses from observations. [Router guards and context diagnostics](reports/strata-tp-fix.md), commits `3f38fd86` and `457507df`, closed a downstream unsafe-index path without establishing the source of the non-finite values.
6. Four post-fix windows narrowed the problem. [Window 1](reports/strata-tp-window.md) passed a long read but did not establish concurrent reuse. [Window 2](reports/strata-tp-window2.md) passed peer-on long-read execution but failed the intended shared-prefix test condition. [Window 3](reports/strata-tp-window3.md) passed sequential 128k reuse at 87.5 warm decode tok/s with FAST probes. [Window 4](reports/strata-tp-window4.md) failed the two-warm-stream condition: one prefix was cold, so the planned R2 comparison did not run.
7. A [copy-cost analysis](reports/strata-tp-slow-review.md) motivated [opt-in TP speed work](reports/strata-tp-speed.md). The [final comparison](reports/strata-tp-final.md) found native R2 3.27× faster on the first matched pair of warm 128k conversations. TP prefetch increased traffic and reduced observed speed. TP's repeated/fairness/sustained qualification remained incomplete. R2's small cache later lost a prefix, so that recipe also needed more work.
8. [Cache tests X1/X2](reports/strata-r2-cache.md) showed that raising bytes alone did not prevent eviction. R3 concurrent prefill reduced writer pauses but failed with non-finite logits and cleared cached state. [X3](reports/strata-r2-x3.md) showed that eight entries still lost an idle third conversation. A [relay scout](reports/strata-prefill-relay.md) found no useful host-traffic saving for a layer split; this was analysis, not a relay hardware trial.
9. The final tested candidates were [four streams at a 128k ceiling](reports/strata-r2-x4.md) and [two streams at a 256k ceiling](reports/strata-r2-x5.md), both with 64 GiB cache and twelve parked entries. Every tested continuation hit. Both filled the entry limit and began evicting. The two-stream test used actual prompts around 200k, not the full 256k ceiling. The later [production application](reports/strata-prod-r2-apply.md) installed two-stream/256k and verified switching to four-stream/128k and back, using sixteen entries. Those were startup and short-prefix checks, not a repeat of the long-context load tests. The historical [R2 recipe reduction](reports/strata-tp-truth-recipe.md) records how R2 was selected before these cache changes.

## Findings at a glance

| Finding | Measured result | Practical limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Shared host-memory slowdown | CPU copy 9.714 → 3.818 → 9.612 GB/s; decode 71.260 → 29.557 → 71.552 tok/s after idle | One rig; physical throttle register/trigger unknown |
| Native R2 versus TP, matched warm pair | 44.78 versus 13.69 whole-request tok/s, 3.27× | One pair; runs traversed different host-state trajectories; cache budgets differed |
| TP single warm stream | 87.5 tok/s in window 3, 87.6 in final A0 | No demonstrated native advantage reversal; no full TP concurrent qualification |
| TP prefetch | Warm 41.1 → 24.9 tok/s; host bytes/card 38.001 → 76.462 GB | Changed host state and reduced resident-expert capacity prevent isolating one cause |
| R3 concurrent prefill | Writer gaps 1.9 / 1.5 s, then non-finite logits at position 127936 | Rejected for this build/workload |
| Four cached 128k histories, X4 | 20/20 continuations hit; whole-request 52.3/53.5 early, 28.7 later tok/s | 12/12 entries, 4 evictions; about 2k context room in tested continuations |
| Two active ~200k histories, X5 | 20/20 continuations hit; single decode 62.9 tok/s; paired decode sum 54.7 early / 53.0 sustained | Whole-request 40.0 / 36.6; 12/12 entries, 8 evictions; idle third not rechecked after sustained load |

## How to reproduce

Read [RECIPES.md](RECIPES.md) for the exact tested settings, [METHOD.md](METHOD.md) for measurement and safety rules, and [FINDINGS.md](FINDINGS.md) for ranked results and limits. Start with a clean native profile and inspect both the launch plan and actual process. A desktop installer alone does not establish the experimental engine identity.

The [driver guide](drivers/README.md) explains the sanitized original window sources and a portable request-only measurement driver. The archive preserves the original orchestration logic for inspection but deliberately cannot execute: installation paths, endpoints and original inputs are omitted. Supply a model at `<model path>`, newly generated synthetic histories, a local endpoint and a rig-specific restore guard. Validate identity, cache reuse, request length, hardware health and host state before comparing rates. New histories cannot reproduce the original greedy hashes.

The reports retain result tables and bounded excerpts. Raw evidence folders, logs, prompts, image fixtures and private deployment configuration are excluded. Advice-only reports say so. Historical recommendations remain historical; the later correctness failures and final cache windows govern the recipes here. The production-apply report became available during export and is included. Its sixteen-entry deployment remains distinct from the twelve-entry long-context load tests; rollback was not exercised. [SANITIZATION.md](SANITIZATION.md) records the scrub and local verification.
